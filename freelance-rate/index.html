How the hourly rate is worked out

Project and fees

How it works

  • Revenue needed = income goal ÷ (1 − set aside %) + business expenses. The set aside is taken from your profit (revenue minus expenses).
  • Billable hours = working weeks × hours per week × billable %.
  • Required hourly rate = revenue needed ÷ billable hours, rounded up to the next cent (or yen). It is a floor, not a market price.
  • Suggested project price = hourly rate × estimated hours × (1 + buffer %).
  • Take-home = price − platform fee − transfer fee. The transfer fee % is taken from what is left after the platform fee, then the fixed fee is added.
  • Effective hourly rate = take-home ÷ estimated hours, and ÷ hours with the buffer if you use all of it.

Example

You want to keep $40,000 a year. You expect $3,000 of business expenses and want to set aside 25% of your profit. $40,000 ÷ 0.75 = $53,333.33, plus $3,000 = $56,333.33 of revenue. You work 48 weeks × 40 hours, but only 60% is billable: 1,152 hours. $56,333.33 ÷ 1,152 = $48.9005, so your required hourly rate is $48.91.

A 40-hour project with a 15% buffer is 46 hours: 46 × $48.91 = $2,249.86. A 10% platform fee takes $224.99, and a 1% transfer fee takes $20.25 of the $2,024.87 left. You keep $2,004.62 — about $50.12 an hour if the job takes 40 hours, but $43.58 if it takes all 46. If your effective rate falls below your required rate, raise the price or cut the hours.
