Invoice due date calculator

Find out when an invoice is due from the invoice date and the payment terms — Net 30, Net 60, EOM, Net 30 EOM, 15th of next month or your own number of days. See how many days are left or how late a payment is, check an early payment discount like 2/10 Net 30, and paste a list of invoices to see what is overdue. For freelancers and small businesses. Nothing you enter is sent anywhere.

The terms in your contract or on your invoice always come first. Some clients read terms like EOM differently, so agree on them in writing. Public holidays are not included — check them yourself. This tool does not give legal or tax advice and does not work out late fees or interest.

1. Invoice and terms

Net 30 means 30 calendar days after the invoice date, weekends included. Many businesses keep the date as it is; some pay on the weekday before or after. Public holidays are not built in — check your country's holidays yourself.

2. Early payment discount

Optional. “2/10 Net 30” means 2% off if paid within 10 days, otherwise the full amount within 30 days. Leave both empty if there is no discount.

How the terms are counted

  • Net 7 / 10 / 15 / 30 / 45 / 60 / 90: that many calendar days after the invoice date. Weekends count.
  • Due on receipt: the invoice date itself.
  • EOM: the last day of the month the invoice is dated in. (Some businesses use “EOM” to mean a later date, so check what your client means.)
  • Net 30 EOM: 30 days after the last day of the invoice month.
  • 15th of next month: the 15th of the month after the invoice month. A December invoice is due on January 15 of the next year.
  • Early payment discount: the discount deadline is counted from the invoice date and is not moved for weekends. Annualized cost = discount % ÷ (100% − discount %) × 365 ÷ (payment period − discount period).

Examples

An invoice for $1,500 is dated Monday, October 5, 2026, on 2/10 Net 30. The full amount is due 30 days later, on Wednesday, November 4, 2026. If the client pays by Thursday, October 15, they pay $1,470.00 and save $30.00. Skipping the discount is like borrowing $1,470 for 20 days at a cost of $30: 2 ÷ 98 × 365 ÷ 20 = about 37.24% a year.

Month ends: January 31, 2027 + Net 30 is March 2, 2027 (February has 28 days), but January 31, 2028 + Net 30 is March 1, 2028 (a leap year). Net 30 EOM on an October 23, 2026 invoice is October 31 + 30 days = November 30, 2026. “15th of next month” on a December 10, 2026 invoice is January 15, 2027.

Weekends: an invoice dated October 1, 2026 on Net 30 lands on Saturday, October 31. Keep the date, or move it to Friday, October 30 or Monday, November 2 — whichever your agreement says.
